Overview

From the tank floor, up the wall,
and through the manhole.

RZ Spider is designed for double bottoms, side tanks and other tight compartments. Four legs splay out in an X, the body fits through a standard manhole, and the arm, sensors and compute all sit on a 620 × 440 × 160 mm body.

  • 12 sealed joint modules

    Three active joints per leg (hip yaw, hip pitch and knee pitch). Hip pitch sits 150 mm directly above hip yaw, so the two axes intersect. Every joint is sealed, with 400 N·m of peak torque.

  • Electro-permanent magnetic feet

    Four independently floating electro-permanent magnet modules per foot, designed for ≥ 1,400 N of holding force that stays on without power.

  • Six-axis cobot arm

    A lightweight 48 V cobot arm with about 850 mm of reach and a quick-change wrist for the spray gun, welding torch, inspection head and other tools.

  • Perception & compute

    A 360° lidar plus front and rear depth cameras, with Jetson Orin NX edge compute.

  • Tethered power

    A 30 m umbilical carries 400 V DC power, optical fiber and a load-bearing core that doubles as a fall-arrest line.

Magnetic climbing

Electro-permanent feet
that hold without power

Each foot carries a 2 × 2 array of electro-permanent magnet modules. Every module floats ±3 mm on its own, and a passive two-axis ankle conforms to weld seams and curved bilges. Hall sensors confirm adhesion in real time: the fourth foot lifts only when three are locked on, and if power drops, the magnets keep holding.

  • ≥ 1,400 N per foot (design target)
  • Keeps holding if power is lost
  • Lifts a foot only when three are locked

Through manholes

Body first,
then the legs one by one

Holding on to the near side of the bulkhead, the robot passes its body through the 600 × 800 mm (R300) manhole first, then straightens each leg through the opening and flips the knee to land on the far side, with at least three feet attached throughout. A sampling planner generates the crossing, an experience library caches solutions for common manholes, and a learned policy carries it out.

  • 600 × 800 mm (R300) manholes
  • At least three feet attached throughout
  • Steps over longitudinals up to 450 mm

Explosion-proof by design

Sealed joints and body,
no exposed motors

Coating inside a tank fills the air with solvent vapor, so RZ Spider is explosion-proof by design. Joints and body are fully sealed with the motors enclosed in their housings, cables run through hollow shafts and leave through Ex cable glands, and the housings close on flame-path joints with bolted flanges.

  • Motors sealed inside their housings
  • Cabling through hollow shafts · Ex cable glands
  • Flame-path joints · bolted flanges

Perception

It sees every stiffener

The 360° lidar sweeps the whole compartment outward from the body in one continuous pass, turning the steel around it into a point cloud. Front and rear depth cameras cover the ground right under the feet, and a voxel occupancy map tells the controller where to step. Global localization registers live point clouds against the hull CAD, and can use survey data from RZ Radar directly.

  • 360° lidar + front and rear depth cameras
  • Voxel occupancy map
  • CAD-based localization ≤ 10 mm (design target)

Reinforcement learning

Thousands of robots
learning in parallel

Thousands of robots train at once in GPU-parallel simulation, on double-bottom cells, longitudinals and manholes generated to real ship dimensions. Wide randomization of adhesion, friction, mass and tether tension keeps the policy stable once it reaches a real tank.

  • 4,096 parallel environments
  • MPC reference motions + learned tracking
  • Randomized adhesion, friction and mass

Arm and body together

Working on the wall,
with every coat on record

With the body locked onto the wall, the six-axis arm follows planned trajectories at a 300–⁠400 mm spray distance with 50% overlap, targeting a 320 µm dry film. Film estimates for every zone are logged with temperature, humidity and dew point to build the PSPC coating technical file.

  • 320 µm target dry film
  • 300–⁠400 mm spray distance
  • PSPC quality records

One robot, many tools

Keep the arm,
swap the tool

Same chassis, same arm, with a quick-change interface at the wrist. A MAG torch lays fillet welds between the bottom plating and the floors; switch to the inspection head and a laser scans the weld profile, an ultrasonic probe measures plate thickness, and a camera with a ring light records it all. Spraying, welding and inspection, from one robot.

  • MAG fillet welding
  • Laser weld profiling · ultrasonic thickness
  • Quick-change at the wrist

Work modules

One platform,
many jobs inside the hull

RZ Spider is a multi-task platform for in-hull work: change the end tool and the same robot takes on a different job.

  1. 01

    Spraying

    An automatic airless gun coats walls and overheads inside the tank.

  2. 02

    Welding

    A quick-change MAG torch lays fillet welds between bottom plating and floors inside double-hull blocks.

  3. 03

    Inspection

    A quick-change inspection head scans weld profiles by laser, measures plate thickness ultrasonically and records images; after coating it measures dry-film thickness and writes the PSPC records.

  4. 04

    Grinding

    Weld-seam grinding and surface preparation before coating.

Work modules will open up as the engineering prototype matures.
