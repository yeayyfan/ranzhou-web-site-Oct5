Wheels & legs

Wheels and legs,
over the ribs

On open floor, RZ Titan rolls at up to 500 mm/s. At a rib frame, its dual-deck, eight-leg mechanism lifts its leg groups in turn to cross obstacles up to 600 mm. Four steering wheels, arranged same-side and diagonally, allow sideways moves and in-place rotation.

  • Obstacles ≤ 600 mm
  • Grade ≤ 10°
  • 500 mm/s

Chassis

Four steering wheels, same-side and diagonal

The steering wheels sit on the same side and across the diagonal, so the robot can move sideways or spin in place to reposition inside narrow compartments.

Magnetic climbing

Up the walls,
through the manholes

Each RZ Spider foot carries four electro-permanent magnet modules, designed for ≥ 1,400 N of holding force that stays on without power. A foot lifts only when the other three are locked, so the robot walks from the tank floor up the wall, then passes 600 × 800 mm manholes body first, with the legs following one by one.

  • ≥ 1,400 N per foot (design target)
  • Holds without power
  • Three feet locked before a lift

Through manholes

Body first,
then the legs one by one

Holding on to the near side of the bulkhead, the robot passes its body through the 600 × 800 mm (R300) manhole first, then straightens each leg through the opening and flips the knee to land on the far side, with at least three feet attached throughout. A sampling planner generates the crossing, an experience library caches solutions for common manholes, and a learned policy carries it out.

  • 600 × 800 mm (R300) manholes
  • At least three feet attached throughout
  • Steps over longitudinals up to 450 mm

Reinforcement learning

Thousands of robots
learning in parallel

Thousands of robots train at once in GPU-parallel simulation, on double-bottom cells, longitudinals and manholes generated to real ship dimensions. Wide randomization of adhesion, friction, mass and tether tension keeps the policy stable once it reaches a real tank.

  • 4,096 parallel environments
  • MPC reference motions + learned tracking
  • Randomized adhesion, friction and mass

Spraying

Precise reach
to every bulkhead

On RZ Titan, a Cartesian gantry carries the nozzle and a multi-axis explosion-proof arm keeps coating quality under precise control across a 0–⁠3.2 m working height. RZ Spider locks onto the wall and sprays along planned paths with its six-axis arm, extending the work into the gaps between frames and tight compartments.

  • 0–⁠3.2 m working height
  • Works directly on walls
  • No blind spots

Arm and body together

Working on the wall,
with every coat on record

With the body locked onto the wall, the six-axis arm follows planned trajectories at a 300–⁠400 mm spray distance with 50% overlap, targeting a 320 µm dry film. Film estimates for every zone are logged with temperature, humidity and dew point to build the PSPC coating technical file.

  • 320 µm target dry film
  • 300–⁠400 mm spray distance
  • PSPC quality records

Survey & perception

Map the tank first,
then send in the robots

RZ Radar scans the compartment and registers it to the hull CAD to produce spray paths and a navigation map, then stays in the tank as a positioning base station while coating is under way. RZ Titan and RZ Spider work from their own lidar and vision, and vision recognition with online film inspection closes the quality loop.

Built-in safety

Designed for
enclosed tanks

Coating inside tanks brings solvent vapor and confined-space risk. RZ Titan has an intrinsically safe, explosion-proof design in a compact body. RZ Spider is explosion-proof by design, with sealed joints and body and no exposed motors; its electro-permanent feet hold without power, and its tether core doubles as a fall-arrest line. RZ Radar is designed to IP65 with an Ex ib IIB T4 protection target.
